Portraits.

Faces are arguments. Every frame either lands a sentence — or it doesn't.

The job isn't to make someone look good.
It's to make the camera listen.

Portraits are the discipline I'm hardest on myself for. There's no plate to re-stage, no skyline to come back to. It's a face, three minutes after meeting a stranger, in a room with the wrong light. You either find them or you don't.

Below — five chapters from the last year. Studio editorial in a small room with one window. Street fashion with a model who knew exactly where to stand. Industrial editorial at a vintage rail yard at golden hour. Behind the lens — once in a while we point the camera back at the people who run the cameras. And field work — brand activations where the portrait has to happen anyway, inside the noise.

01 The Studio · Editorial

A room with one window.

A small studio, a deep red velvet sofa, dark teal panels. The lighting was the room — pulled apart with one shaped flag and patience.

The sitter chose the dress. The dress chose the lighting. The room — borrowed for a Sunday afternoon — already had the panels and the sofa; we moved one chair and closed two blinds. Everything else is restraint.

The fourth frame is the one that ran. The other three are the ones that earned it.
